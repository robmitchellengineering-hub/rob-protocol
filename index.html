01 · The problem

Wi-Fi wasn't built for sound.

Radio travel time is 0.3 µs per 100 m, and stereo PCM uses under 1% of a Wi-Fi 6 link. The 5–50 ms you actually hear isn't physics — it's scheduling, queues and retries.

That's a protocol problem. And protocol problems can be fixed.

02 · The packet

One packet, engineered for air.

ROB rides RTP (RFC 3550) over UDP, so standard tools — Wireshark, and every AES67 device — understand it. Everything ROB adds lives in one small header extension and a payload of layered audio blocks.

03 · Layered redundancy

Graceful degradation, not silence.

On Wi-Fi every packet costs the same airtime whatever its size — so bytes are cheap and packet rate is the scarce resource. ROB never sends extra packets. It layers protection inside each one.

A lost packet is rebuilt from the next packet at 16-bit, and from the one after that at 10-bit. Bit depth dips for a few milliseconds; the show goes on.

04 · Multipath

Two paths, one stream.

Identical packets travel two radios, two access points, or cable + Wi-Fi at once. The receiver keeps the first copy of every sequence number — full quality, zero added latency.

SMPTE 2022-7 semantics, over the air.

05 · Clock

Every sample, on one clock.

Every sample carries its capture time on a shared network clock. Every output plays it back at capture time + latency — so wired and wireless stay sample-aligned.

PTPv2 where a grandmaster exists · ROB-Sync's two-way time transfer everywhere else.

06 · Soft slots

Never send late audio.

Nodes share one clock and take turns within the packet cycle, so they stop transmitting at the same instant. A packet that can no longer arrive in time is dropped from its own queue.

Stale packets never steal airtime from fresh ones.

07 · Latency tiers

One protocol, every distance.

Every path gets the lowest latency its link can honestly hold — and every output stays aligned to the same clock.
